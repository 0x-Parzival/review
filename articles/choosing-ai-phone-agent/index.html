PRACTICAL GUIDE

What to check before choosing an AI phone agent

Start with the calls your business actually receives. A good demonstration matters less than clear answers about scope, costs, failures, and the work your team still owns.

By Keshav Baliyan · Published October 3, 2026

Ownership disclosure

The author owns Spiritual AI, 21 Days, and AI Helper. Product descriptions are based on public pages checked on October 3, 2026. This is not an independent benchmark or a verified customer review.

On this page
  1. Define one call workflow
  2. Ask for the full cost

  4. Check the records and privacy

Define one call workflow

Write down the five most common reasons people call. Choose one routine workflow for the first pilot, such as answering opening-hours questions or collecting a callback request. Specify the information the agent may use and the promises it must not make.

Keep inbound answering separate from outbound calling. Ask who can start outbound calls, how permission is recorded, and how the contact and purpose are selected. Confirm applicable calling requirements with your provider and appropriate local guidance before rollout.

Ask for the full cost

  • What is the billing interval?
  • How many minutes or calls are included?
  • Are phone numbers and provider charges separate?
  • Which destination countries are supported?
  • What happens at the usage limit?
  • Does setup, support, or integration cost extra?

For example, AI Helper’s public pricing asks customers to confirm the interval, call volume, and telephony charges. A headline subscription figure alone is not enough for a cost comparison.

Test the awkward calls

Prepare sample conversations for an unclear request, incorrect business information, a frustrated caller, a language change, and a request that needs a person. Check whether the agent admits uncertainty and follows the boundaries you supplied.

Ask the vendor to explain connection failures, interrupted calls, unavailable services, and escalation. Do not assume a transfer or integration exists because the agent sounds conversational.

Check the records and privacy

Ask which transcripts, recordings, summaries, and contact details are stored; how long they are retained; who can access them; and how deletion works. Confirm whether a recording is actually available through your phone provider.

Make AI disclosure part of the greeting. Decide what sensitive information callers should avoid providing and which cases should be routed to a person. Evaluate the vendor’s actual terms and privacy information rather than relying on a marketing description.

Run a small pilot before expanding

Set a limited call volume and review the results. Track whether callers got the correct information, how often a person was needed, whether summaries captured the request, and the actual cost. Choose acceptance criteria before the pilot so you do not judge success only by an impressive demo.
